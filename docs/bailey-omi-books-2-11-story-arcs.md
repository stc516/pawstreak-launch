**Creative guardrails:** S.T. Carroll; children ages 3–7; Bailey is the smaller curious black-and-white blue-eyed female Husky with green collar, Omi/Meiomi is the older golden-tan mixed rescue with pointed ears and pink collar. Keep warm watercolor and colored-pencil treatment, accurate markings/relative size, grown-up supervision and safe dog behavior. Let the sisters alternately lead, learn, and be surprised. The priority order below is a working editorial ranking, not a commitment to publish.

## Series strategy

1. **First four (#2–#5):** Vary pacing and mood: fast outdoor comedy → sensory surprise → disagreement → quiet wonder. This is an editorial arc, not an in-world timeline.
2. **Second four (#6–#9):** Expand season and place, and deepen each dog's independence: nature care → asking for help → adaptation → community manners.
3. **Final two (#10–#11):** Pair outward curiosity with restraint and emotional homecoming. Consider whether #11 might eventually be a season finale rather than simply the eleventh title.
4. **Book 2 only:** Green-light the actual location and a specific real memory; then write a 10–14 beat outline and 24/32-page turn map. Do not commission final art for any unapproved concept.
5. **Reader connection:** Each future published book can offer one safe age-appropriate family noticing activity on its own public story page, with no invented app achievement.

## Quality and uniqueness test before commissioning a manuscript
- Can a parent describe the problem in one sentence?
- Does the story change how Bailey and Omi understand each other, rather than repeat “Bailey is scared; Omi helps”?
- Is the main action safe for real dogs and respectful of other people?
- Is there a surprising, legible picture-book page turn?
- Is the visual palette and setting distinct from prior books?
- Is the real-life inspiration marked as verified rather than inferred?
- Does the story stand alone for a new reader aged 3–7?
