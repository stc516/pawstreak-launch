# Bailey & Omi Adventures — Book 3 development treatment
**Working title:** *Omi's Unexpected Splash*  
**Prepared:** November 12, 2026 · **Author identity:** S.T. Carroll  
**State:** Original, UNAPPROVED editorial candidate. Not a published title, canonical manuscript, commissioned illustration, or a claim that this event happened to the real dogs.

## Creative premise
For ages 3–7. Usually it is older sister Omi who knows what to do. On a gentle, leash-friendly waterfront walk, a little splash surprises her and suddenly she wants more space. Bailey, who knows what it feels like to hesitate, models patience without announcing a grand lesson. Omi chooses what feels comfortable, on her terms. The sisters' roles reverse without either dog becoming a joke.

**One-sentence pitch:** The confident big sister gets an unexpected surprise at the water's edge, and the little sister shows her how to take a pause.

## Continuity, safety, and differentiation
- Bailey: smaller black-and-white, blue-eyed female Husky, green collar; observant, curious, sometimes tentative. She does not become magically fearless.
- Omi/Meiomi: larger golden-tan rescue with large pointed ears, pink collar; steady, protective, older-sister energy. Surprise is momentary, not a trait of cowardice.
- Maggie: caring adult handler who keeps both dogs leashed, watches body language, allows distance, and never forces water interaction.
- Location is **fictionalized pending research**: only choose a real dog-legal, sheltered shoreline or lakeside walking path after confirming access and leash policy. Keep both dogs on a stable dry path. No dock edges, swimming, rough surf, unleashed retrieval, wildlife chase, or unsafe children/dog interactions.
- Unlike Book 1's staircase and Book 2's windy park, Book 3 belongs visually to blue-green reflections and cream-tan wet paw prints. The emotional movement is *Omi accepts help* rather than *Bailey learns courage* or *both chase the wind*.
- The comedic beat is a tiny, surprising ripple and Omi's dignified wet expression; **never** depict a deliberate scare, spraying an anxious dog, or laughter at distress.

## Manuscript direction / line edit notes
- Aim for roughly 350–450 words across the ten story spreads, read aloud in about 4–5 minutes. The quoted text above is **draft beat copy**, not finished manuscript.
- Keep sound words rare: one “Plip!” is enough to create the page-turn payoff.
- Allow the visual joke to do the work; do not explain the reflection gag in three sentences.
- Avoid positioning Bailey as an expert or Maggie as training a fearful dog. Maggie merely provides space and supervision.
- A child should understand this story even without Book 1: Omi is usually steady; today she is startled; Bailey helps by waiting.
- If the actual dog-safe waterside setting cannot be verified, retain the emotional arc and move the entire story to a rainy park path with a puddle. **Do not** invent a real-life memory or label the setting as real.
