# Bailey & Omi Adventures — Book 2 exploratory story beats
**Working title:** *Bailey & Omi and the Windy-Day Chase* · **S.T. Carroll** · **Prepared:** November 5, 2026
**State:** Fictional early-development outline only. **Not** a manuscript, approved real-life recollection, commissioned art, KDP change, or announced release. Follows the [ranked Books 2–11 plan](./bailey-omi-books-2-11-story-arcs.md).

## Story promise (one sentence)
At a breezy, supervised coastal park picnic, Omi follows what moves while Bailey watches **where** it moves—and their very different instincts finally bring them together.

## Character and art lock
- **Bailey:** smaller black-and-white female Husky puppy, bright blue eyes, green collar; inquisitive, observant and sometimes unsure.
- **Omi (Meiomi):** larger older golden-tan mixed rescue with tall pointed ears, pink collar; practiced, caring, but not automatically right.
- **Maggie:** the familiar grown-up supervisor, consistent with Book 1 design; holds leash control, ensures safe distance from traffic and wildlife, handles the actual dropped item.
- Approved warm watercolor/colored-pencil styling; believable paws, body proportions and consistent markings. Breezy coastal sage, pale sea blue, sandstone cream, soft coral accents. Do not generate fake “Book 2 pages” for a Book 1 ad.
- **Physical/safety note:** Dogs stay leashed and with Maggie. They do not eat/grab picnic litter, chase objects toward water/roads, or separate from their guardian. Any apparent “chase” is a playful observation game, not a runaway-dog plot. Use a lightweight reusable picnic cloth secured on grass; Maggie alone retrieves it.

## 10 provisional picture-book spreads (story spreads; front/back matter separate)
| Beat | Story action | Page-turn / illustration hook | Emotional shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The sisters arrive at a leashed, dog-appropriate grass picnic area. Bailey sniffs each blade; Omi scans the bustling sky. | A long ribbon of breeze runs from Bailey's nose to the edge of the page. | Eager anticipation |
| 2 | Maggie sets out a picnic cloth and holds the leashes. A puff of wind tickles Bailey's ears and lifts Omi's tan fur. | Both dogs' silhouettes expand into comical fluffy shapes. | First laugh |
| 3 | A stronger gust lifts one loose corner of the cloth. Maggie keeps other belongings secure. Omi thinks the flutter means “go!” | Close-up of Omi's front paw hovering before she pauses on cue. | Impulse checked |
| 4 | Omi watches a leaf tumble one way and confidently predicts where the cloth will billow next. She's wrong. | Turn: leaf circles behind Omi instead of continuing ahead. | Surprise |
| 5 | Bailey notices the grass bending and a second leaf looping in an odd little circle. She pauses, then looks upwind. | Three tiny leaf paths in a watercolor swirl, readable to a child. | Curious discovery |
| 6 | Omi would rather keep looking where the first leaf went; Bailey looks to Maggie for a chance to show her idea. | Split composition: Omi on one side, Bailey on the other; both within Maggie's leashes. | Brief frustration |
| 7 | Maggie stays with them while Bailey watches the wind. The fluttering corner returns nearly to its starting place. | A quiet half-page pause, followed by a huge “there it is!” visual reveal. | Insight |
| 8 | Omi spots the returning cloth first and politely waits while Maggie presses it flat and secures it. | Omi's confident eyes and Bailey's delighted blue-eyed look converge. | Shared success |
| 9 | The wind tries one last silly trick: it makes both dogs' ears stand at improbable angles. Maggie laughs. | Double-page joy with gust ribbons but no dangerous flying debris. | Relief and humor |
| 10 | They enjoy a calm snack/relaxation time away from human food, close together; Bailey and Omi both watch the sky now. | Echo Beat 1's view, but the sisters are now seated side by side. | Togetherness |

## Provisional repeating read-aloud line
“Whoosh went the wind. Which way would it go?” Repeat three times, with each recurrence changing the sisters' answer. **Original draft phrase only**, not quoted Book 1 text. Test for natural read-aloud pacing at 3–7 years.

## Editorial test against Book 1
- **Distinct engine:** outdoor light comedy plus teamwork (not a staircase bravery retry).
- **Balanced agency:** Bailey earns the insight; Omi models patience, observation and working together; Maggie maintains safety.
- **Visual escalation:** gentle swirl → leaf misdirection → half-page pause → returning-cloth reveal → funny ears.
- **Heart:** Omi acknowledges Bailey's idea by staying close and sharing attention; avoid making either dog a generic moral spokesperson.
- **Seasonality:** generic fictional coastal park, **not** a named real park or documented personal memory.
