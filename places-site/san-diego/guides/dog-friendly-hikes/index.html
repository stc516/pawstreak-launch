7 dog-friendly hikes worth leaving the neighborhood for.

San Diego gives dog people a lot more than sidewalks and dog parks. These seven hikes span coastal lagoons, canyon trails, oak woodland and mountain meadows. Most require leashes, and summer heat matters more for dogs than humans.

Important: Trail rules vary by land manager. Check current regulations before driving out, keep dogs leashed where required, carry water, and skip exposed inland trails during hot weather.
01 · ESCONDIDO

Elfin Forest Recreational Reserve

Moderate to moderately difficult · roughly 3–7 miles. A bigger adventure with climbs, reservoir views and weekday voice-control allowances on some upper trails. The Way Up Trail still requires leashes.

02 · DEL MAR

San Dieguito Lagoon

Easy · roughly 3–4 miles. Smooth dirt, boardwalk and lagoon scenery leading toward Del Mar’s North Beach. Excellent when you want a coastal walk instead of a formal hike.

03 · CARLSBAD

Batiquitos Lagoon

Easy · roughly 3 miles. A flat, scenic lagoon-side trail and one of North County’s most popular everyday dog walks. Dogs remain on leash.

04 · IMPERIAL BEACH

Beach Trail / Tijuana River Trail

Easy · roughly 1 mile. A short coastal option south of Seacoast Drive with beach scenery and access toward the Tijuana River mouth.

05 · POWAY

Blue Sky Ecological Reserve

Moderate to moderately strenuous · roughly 3–6 miles. Oak woodland with options toward Lake Poway and Ramona Lake. Best as a cool-morning outing; inland heat can build fast.

06 · LAGUNA MOUNTAINS

Sunset Trail

Easy · roughly 3 miles. Meadows, forest shade and a very different feel from coastal San Diego. A strong warm-season escape when lower-elevation trails are too exposed.

07 · RANCHO PEÑASQUITOS

Los Peñasquitos Canyon

Easy to moderate · roughly 3–7 miles. Gentle terrain, creek crossings and enough distance flexibility to make this anything from a morning walk to a longer outing.

Two easy city dog-walk options

The City of San Diego also specifically calls out Navajo Canyon and Maple Canyon as good dog-walking areas with wide access paths. Dogs must remain leashed in city canyons and open-space parks.
